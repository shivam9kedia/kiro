### Methodology: How We Gathered This Data

Our dataset represents 1,000+ residential water tests conducted over a 24-month period across 38 states. Each test analyzed between 15 and 50+ parameters depending on the testing package, including heavy metals, minerals, bacteria, volatile organic compounds, and aesthetic parameters.

**Sample demographics:**
- 58% municipal/city water users
- 42% private well owners
- Geographic distribution weighted toward Southeast, Midwest, and Southwest
- Mix of urban (35%), suburban (40%), and rural (25%) homes
- Home ages ranging from new construction to 100+ years old

**Testing methodology:**
- First-draw samples (water sitting in pipes 6+ hours) for lead and copper
- Running samples for general chemistry parameters
- Certified laboratory analysis using EPA-approved methods
- Results compared against EPA Maximum Contaminant Levels (MCLs), EPA Secondary Standards, and Health Advisory Levels

### Municipal Water vs. Well Water: The Real Comparison

One of the most common questions we receive is whether city water or well water is "better." Our data provides a nuanced answer:

**Municipal water strengths:**
- 0% bacteria detection (vs. 38% in well water)
- Consistent pH range (7.0-8.5 in 94% of municipal samples)
- Lower iron and manganese levels
- Regulated and monitored continuously

**Municipal water weaknesses:**
- Higher lead detection rate (38% vs. 26% in well water) — due to older distribution infrastructure and service lines
- Universal chlorine/chloramine presence (creates disinfection byproducts)
- Higher PFAS detection rates in communities near industrial sites
- Fluoride addition (a concern for some households)

**Well water strengths:**
- No disinfection byproducts
- No added fluoride (unless naturally present)
- Often lower TDS in certain aquifer types
- Owner has complete control over treatment decisions

**Well water weaknesses:**
- 38% tested positive for coliform bacteria
- Significantly higher hardness (average 245 ppm vs. 168 ppm municipal)
- Higher iron and manganese prevalence
- 6% exceeded nitrate MCL (vs. 1% municipal)
- No regulatory monitoring—problems go undetected without proactive testing

**The conclusion:** Neither source is categorically "better." Each has predictable strengths and weaknesses that require different treatment approaches.

### Regional Patterns: Geography Matters More Than You Think

Our data revealed dramatic regional variations that explain why no single water treatment recommendation works nationwide:

**Southeast (FL, GA, SC, NC, AL):**
- Highest average hardness: 220 ppm
- Highest sulfur/hydrogen sulfide prevalence: 28% of tests
- Iron bacteria most common in this region
- Hurricane-related contamination events visible in seasonal data

**Midwest (OH, IN, MI, WI, MN, IL):**
- Highest lead detection rate: 42% of homes with detectable lead
- Agricultural nitrate impact: 11% above MCL
- Extreme hardness in limestone aquifer regions (200-400+ ppm)
- Atrazine and agricultural herbicide detection in rural wells

**Southwest (AZ, NM, TX, NV):**
- Highest TDS levels: Average 410 ppm
- Arsenic naturally present in 15% of well samples
- Fluoride naturally above 4 ppm in 8% of wells
- Extreme hardness in some aquifer systems

**Northeast (NY, NJ, PA, CT, MA):**
- Highest radon-in-water detection
- Lead from oldest housing stock in the nation
- Lower hardness overall (average 95 ppm)
- PFAS contamination hotspots near military installations

### The Most Dangerous Finding: What People Don't Know

Perhaps the most concerning pattern in our data isn't any single contaminant—it's the gap between perception and reality.

**Pre-test perception vs. reality:**
- 71% of homeowners rated their water quality as "good" or "excellent" before testing
- Of those confident respondents, 68% had at least one parameter exceeding health guidelines
- 89% of homes with detectable lead had occupants unaware of lead presence
- 94% of well owners with bacteria had experienced no symptoms (yet)

This perception gap exists because:
1. Many contaminants are tasteless, odorless, and invisible (lead, nitrates, bacteria at low levels)
2. Municipal water quality reports test at the plant, not at your tap
3. Well owners often haven't tested since the well was drilled
4. Aesthetic parameters (hardness, iron) mask awareness of invisible health concerns

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does my water compare to the national averages in this study?
The only way to compare your specific water to our dataset averages is through testing. National averages mask enormous local variation—a home in Phoenix faces completely different challenges than one in Philadelphia. A comprehensive water test from Wellness Water Company provides your individual results alongside context about how your parameters compare regionally and nationally.

### Why do so many homes have detectable lead if it's regulated?
Lead in home tap water primarily comes from building plumbing (lead solder used until 1986, brass fixtures, and lead service lines connecting homes to water mains) rather than from the treatment plant itself. Utilities test at a sampling of homes, and the EPA action level allows some lead to pass regulatory thresholds. First-draw testing at your specific tap reveals your actual exposure.

### Are these results worse than official EPA reports suggest?
Our data reflects point-of-use reality—what comes out of your faucet—rather than what leaves a treatment plant. EPA compliance monitoring measures water quality within the distribution system, which doesn't account for individual building plumbing contribution. Our higher lead and copper detection rates specifically reflect this "last mile" problem between the water main and your glass.

### Should I be alarmed by these findings?
Informed, not alarmed. The vast majority of parameters we detect are treatable with readily available, affordable technology. The real risk isn't the contamination itself—it's living with contamination you don't know about. Testing transforms an unknown risk into a known, manageable condition. Most water quality issues have straightforward, cost-effective solutions once properly identified.
