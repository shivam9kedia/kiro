## PART 1 — EDITORIAL & FACT-CHECK NOTES

**How to use the citations in this document:**
- Each authoritative source should be added as an **outbound hyperlink** on the recommended anchor text inside the article. Outbound links to EPA, CDC, USGS, and peer-reviewed studies strengthen E-E-A-T and help with AI Overviews.
- Link out **once per source per article** (don't over-link the same domain).
- For health articles (#1, #4, #5, #10, #16, #17), keep the "Medically reviewed by Dr. Ronald Sidney Stuart, M.D." stamp and add the review date at publish time.

**Verified corrections already applied to the content file:**
- Article #3: the private-well figure was set to "more than 15 million U.S. households (CDC)." Note that estimates vary by source and year — CDC cites 15M+ households (~43M people); EPA's more recent page cites 23M+ households. Use the CDC figure with the CDC citation, or update to EPA's figure with its citation — both are defensible.

**Numbers stated qualitatively (intentional):** Cost figures ("thousands over ten years," "hundreds of dollars a year on bottled water"), appliance-lifespan reductions, and efficiency losses are described directionally rather than with invented precise dollar amounts, because they vary by household, region, and hardness. This is accurate and defensible; if you want hard numbers, localize them per market and cite the source.

**Brand claims to keep truthful:** "NASA-grade," "lifetime warranty," "physician on the team," and "free water test" are repeated throughout. Ensure each remains accurate to current company offerings before publishing, since they are central E-E-A-T signals.
